# Adjustable laptop riser

A laptop stand I designed to make working more comfortable by raising the laptop and allowing it to sit at different viewing angles.

**Printable model:** https://www.printables.com/model/1929388-laptop_stand

## About the project

I wanted to design a laptop stand that could be adjusted to different heights without taking up too much space on a desk. I made this model using Autodesk Fusion 360 and kept all the parts in one design file. the stand has a flat platform for the laptop, two arms to support it and a base to keep it in place. I also added a hinge arrangement so the platform could be set at different angles.

## What I made

- A platform measuring 280 × 220 mm to hold the laptop
- Six ventilation slots in the platform to leave space for air to pass through
- A 12 mm front edge to help stop the laptop from sliding
- Two support arms connecting the platform to the base
- Two base feet to keep the stand steady on the desk
- Hinge connectors with pivot holes and locking pins for angle adjustment
- Rubber grip pads for the areas that come into contact with the laptop and desk
- Covers for the hinge areas to give the parts a cleaner finish

## Assembly instructions

The parts are intended to be fitted together in this order

1. Place the two base feet in position and fit the anti slip pads underneath
2. Connect the support arms to the base using the hinge connectors and pivot pins
3. Position the hinge connectors for the laptop platform
4. Place the platform above the support arms and line up the pivot holes
5. Fit the pivot pins and locking pins in their intended positions
6. Add the rubber grip pads and hinge covers
7. Check the alignment of the parts and make sure the stand sits properly

The full assembly and folding movement have not yet been tested, so these steps describe the planned fitting order rather than a fully tested assembly process.

## Design checks

I used temporary copies of the parts to check how the hinge arrangement fit together. I also looked at the locking hole positions and available space at the four planned angles of 15°, 25°, 35° and 45°.
The platform, support arms, connectors and base feet did not overlap in the configurations I checked. The complete assembly.

## What I learned

I spent time designing each part and making sure its size and position matched the other parts. The hinge area needed particular attention because the platform and support arms have to line up properly.

I also had to think about small details such as the front stopper, ventilation slots and rubber pads. This project gave me a better understanding of how separate parts can be designed to work together as one product.
